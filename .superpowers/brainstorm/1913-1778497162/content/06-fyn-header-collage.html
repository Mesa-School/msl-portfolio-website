D1 — Diagonal Split (your idea)
Composition: 5:2 banner. Left ~58% = master (header.jpg, FYNERGY hands+drinks). Right ~42% = product hero (Hero.webp, packaging). Diagonal slash separates them.

D1 · Diagonal Split

Master image dominates left, product hero "cuts in" from the right via a clean diagonal. The two blue palettes (sky blue + studio dark blue) create natural color tension. Most editorial.

Pros: single 5:2 footprint · brand wordmark stays prominent · product gets equal credit · most "designed"

D2 — Diagonal Cross (4 quadrants)
Composition: 5:2 banner cut by a single diagonal. Top-left = brand master. Bottom-right = the 5-person team photo. "Brand vs People" duality.

D2 · Diagonal Cross

Single diagonal cut, full half-and-half. Top-left = master brand image, bottom-right = the team. Tells the "polished brand × real people behind it" story in one frame.

Pros: most literal "half and half cross diagonally" · narrative is clear · works for every startup (master + team always exist)

D3 — Master + Diagonal Polaroid Strip
Composition: 5:2 banner unchanged. 3 small "polaroids" tilted diagonally in the bottom-right: product, founder pitching, team. Adds depth without breaking the master.

D3 · Master + Polaroid Strip

Master banner stays untouched. 3 tilted polaroids float in the bottom-right corner — product, founder on stage, team. Playful, founder-personal, less "designed-by-committee" feel.

Pros: banner stays sacred · polaroids show "real" moments · easy to swap photos per startup

My recommendation: D2 (Diagonal Cross). It's the most literal interpretation of your "half and half cross diagonally" idea, and the brand-vs-team duality tells the most powerful story. D1 is a strong second if you want to keep the focus more product-led. D3 is if you want the most casual / human energy.

Implementation note: all three use CSS clip-path for the diagonal — fully responsive, no image baking required. Different startups can use any 2 photos — just need a "master" + a "secondary" tagged in the JSON.
